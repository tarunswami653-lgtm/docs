Turbo's levels are built around a vertical stack of four horizontal platform rows, each presenting its own hazards, collectibles, and opportunities. Understanding how these rows relate to one another — and how the game transitions between screens — is essential to mastering your run. Whether you're just starting out or pushing for a high score, knowing the layout gives you a decisive edge.

## The Four-Tier Platform Layout

Every level screen is divided into four distinct horizontal rows, stacked from top to bottom. Each row consists of a **LEFT segment** and a **RIGHT segment** with a **CENTER GAP** between them. You must navigate these gaps carefully, as falling through at the wrong moment can end your run.

| Row Position | Name | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1st (top) | Top Row | Empty — no items |
| 2nd | Upper-Middle Row | Turbo Soda spawns here |
| 3rd | Lower-Middle Row | Turbo Chips spawn here |
| 4th (bottom) | Bottom Row | **BLANK** — no items, safe zone |

### Top Row
The topmost platform is empty at the start of each level. As difficulty increases, items may begin appearing here, turning what was once a breather into a new challenge.

### Upper-Middle Row
This row is the primary location for **Turbo Soda** items. Collecting or clearing these contributes to your score and triggers level progression events.

### Lower-Middle Row
The lower-middle row hosts **Turbo Chips**. These items often require different handling than Turbo Soda, so pay close attention to the visual differences between them.

### Bottom Row
The bottom row is the blank, floor-level platform. It contains no items and acts as your starting position and safe landing area throughout the level.

<Note>
  The **blank bottom wooden platform** is the lowest row in every level — it is completely empty with no Turbo Soda or Turbo Chips present. This row serves as your safe zone and starting area. When in doubt, returning to the bottom row gives you a moment to reset and plan your next move.
</Note>

## Level Progression Flow

As you clear items from the middle rows, the level advances and new screens are introduced. Each new screen can bring additional item spawns, faster movement, and tighter gaps to navigate.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start on the Bottom Row">
    Every level begins with you positioned on the blank bottom wooden platform. Take a moment to survey the rows above — identify where the Turbo Soda and Turbo Chips are located before you move.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear the Lower-Middle Row">
    Work your way up to the lower-middle row and collect or clear the Turbo Chips. Managing this row first reduces the immediate threats closest to your starting position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear the Upper-Middle Row">
    Move up to the upper-middle row to collect the Turbo Soda items. Be mindful of the center gap — mistiming a jump here can send you back to the bottom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate the Top Row">
    Once the middle rows are cleared, advance to the top row. Early levels leave this row empty, but later levels will spawn items here as well.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trigger Screen Transition">
    Clearing the required items triggers a screen transition, moving you to the next level layout. The difficulty increases with each new screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adapt to New Spawns">
    On the new screen, blank platforms — including the bottom row — may begin filling with items as the game's difficulty scaling kicks in. Stay alert and adjust your strategy accordingly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Increasing Difficulty

As you progress through levels, the game introduces several difficulty modifiers:

- **Item spawn rate increases** — platforms that were previously blank begin filling with new items more quickly.
- **Gap timing tightens** — the center gap between LEFT and RIGHT segments becomes more punishing to navigate.
- **New item types appear** — higher levels introduce items beyond Turbo Soda and Turbo Chips, each with unique behaviors.
- **Platform states change faster** — occupied platforms cycle back to blank and refill more rapidly, giving you less time to react.

Understanding the four-tier layout and using the blank bottom row as your anchor point is the foundation of consistent, high-scoring play in Turbo.
