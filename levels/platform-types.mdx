Every platform row in Turbo is made up of the same fundamental building blocks: a left segment, a right segment, and the center gap that divides them. Knowing how each segment type behaves — and being able to read whether a platform is blank or occupied at a glance — is critical to navigating levels efficiently and avoiding costly mistakes.

## Segment Anatomy

Each horizontal row is split into three zones from left to right:

- **LEFT Segment** — the wooden platform section on the left side of the screen where you can land and stand.
- **CENTER GAP** — the open space in the middle of the row. There is no platform here; falling into the gap drops you down to the next row below.
- **RIGHT Segment** — the wooden platform section on the right side of the screen, mirroring the left segment.

The center gap is consistent across all rows and all level screens. You must jump across it deliberately — there is no platform to catch you in the middle.

## Platform Types Reference

The following table summarizes every platform type you will encounter across Turbo's levels, including which rows they appear in and their key properties.

| Platform Type | Location | Contains Items | Safe to Land On | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blank Bottom Platform | Bottom row (row 4) | No | Yes | Starting position and safe zone; empty floor-level row |
| Blank Top Platform | Top row (row 1) | No (early levels) | Yes | May fill with items in higher difficulty levels |
| Occupied — Turbo Soda | Upper-middle row (row 2) | Yes — Turbo Soda | Yes | Clearing items here contributes to score |
| Occupied — Turbo Chips | Lower-middle row (row 3) | Yes — Turbo Chips | Yes | Different collectible behavior from Turbo Soda |
| Transitioning Platform | Any row | Partially cleared | Yes | Platform mid-state between occupied and blank |
| High-Difficulty Spawn | Any row (late levels) | Yes — new item types | Yes | Appears on previously blank rows as difficulty increases |

## Row-by-Row Breakdown

### Top Row (Row 1)
The top row uses the same LEFT/GAP/RIGHT segment structure as every other row. In early levels it is blank — no items are present, making it a secondary safe zone above the action. As difficulty scales, new items begin spawning here, transforming it from a refuge into another row you must actively manage.

### Upper-Middle Row (Row 2)
This row is consistently occupied by **Turbo Soda** items in the early and mid-game. The visual appearance of the platform changes when items are present: you will see the item object resting on top of the wooden segment. Once cleared, the segment returns to its blank wooden state.

### Lower-Middle Row (Row 3)
The lower-middle row mirrors the upper-middle row in structure but hosts **Turbo Chips** instead of Turbo Soda. Visually distinguish between the two item types by their shape and color cues — confusing them under pressure is a common beginner mistake.

### Bottom Row (Row 4)
The bottom row is the **blank wooden floor-level platform**. It never contains items in standard gameplay at the start of a level. Both the LEFT and RIGHT segments are present as usual, and the CENTER GAP still exists — but there are no collectibles, hazards, or spawned objects on this row. It is your starting point and your fallback position throughout each level.

## Blank vs. Occupied Platform States

Platforms in Turbo have two primary visual states:

**Blank State**
- The wooden platform surface is clear and unobstructed.
- No items sit on top of the segment.
- Safe to land on and move across freely.
- Represented by the plain wooden plank texture.

**Occupied State**
- An item (Turbo Soda, Turbo Chips, or a higher-level variant) rests visibly on the platform segment.
- You must interact with or avoid the item depending on your current objective.
- The platform itself remains safe to land on; the item on top is what you need to manage.

## Center Gap Mechanics

The center gap is not merely decorative — it is an active gameplay element. Key properties of the gap include:

- **No platform exists** in the gap zone. Any movement into the gap without sufficient jump height or momentum results in a fall.
- **Falling through the gap** drops you to the row directly below. From the bottom row, falling through the gap has the most severe consequences since there is no lower platform to catch you.
- **Gap width is consistent** across all rows in a given level, but may vary slightly between level screens as difficulty increases.
- **Jumping across the gap** from the LEFT segment to the RIGHT segment (or vice versa) is a core movement skill. Practice timing your jumps relative to your horizontal momentum to clear the gap cleanly.

Understanding the interplay between LEFT segments, RIGHT segments, and the CENTER GAP — and being able to instantly read whether a platform is blank or occupied — forms the mechanical foundation of every decision you make in Turbo.
